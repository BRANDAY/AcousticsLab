# M4 — auralization: impulse-response synthesis, WAV export, convolution reverb, A/B listening

## Plan

1. **Core, `Results/IRSynthesizer`** (SPEC §2.9). `FIRSynthesisSettings { SampleRate 48000, Seed, PeakDbFS −3, TruncateBelowDb −60, MaxSeconds (0 = histogram length) }`, `FIRImpulse { TimeSeconds, Energy }` for deterministic contributions, `FSynthesizedIR { SampleRate, Samples (mono float), NormalizationGain, PeakBeforeNormalization, EnergyBeforeNormalization, NumBinsUsed, TruncationSeconds, bTruncatedByLevel, NumImpulses }`. `FIRSynthesizer::Synthesize(histogram, impulses, settings)`: for every bin a white-noise burst (PCG32 stream `(Seed, bin)`, uniform in [−1, 1)) scaled so that the sum of squares over the bin equals the bin energy (energy-exact, not just in expectation), deterministic impulses inserted as single samples of amplitude `sqrt(E)` at `round(t·fs)` with their energy removed from the bin they fall in (the ray tracer already adds the direct sound to the histogram, SPEC §2.2.7, so it would otherwise be counted twice), truncation at the first bin where the Schroeder curve falls below `TruncateBelowDb` (never beyond `MaxSeconds` / the histogram), peak normalization to `PeakDbFS`. `SynthesizeForReceiver(FReceiverResult, settings)` builds the impulse list from `DirectArrivalSeconds` / `DirectEnergy`. Band 0 only in v1; the six-band version will need per-band noise filtering (noted in the header).

## Deviations from SPEC / PROMPTS

- The noise of a bin is scaled so that its sum of squares equals the bin energy exactly (not only in expectation): the energy envelope then follows the histogram bit for bit, which makes the test strict; the direct sound (and later any early reflection passed as `FIRImpulse`) is removed from the noise of its bin because the ray tracer already deposits it there (SPEC §2.2.7).
- The convolution normalization is set per response: `UAudioImpulseResponse::NormalizationVolumeDb = -10 log10(sum of squares) + ExtraGainDb`, so the convolved clip has about the loudness of the dry clip whatever the room (a 4.7 s concrete tail carries 30 times the energy of the direct sound; with the engine default of -24 dB the Ray variant would be much louder than Dry).
- A/B is a mix switch, not a re-routing of the sound: the clip always plays into `SM_Auralization`, Dry sets the submix dry level 1 / wet level 0, Ray sets 0 / 1 (`USoundSubmix::SetSubmixDryLevel / SetSubmixWetLevel`). The convolution keeps running in both states, so the switch has no latency and no click. Variants 3-6 keep the current routing and log a note.
- The clip is not spatialized (2D): the synthesized response already carries the direct sound and the 1/d^2 attenuation of the selected receiver, so a spatialized source would count the direct sound twice. Listener = the selected receiver, not the camera; the soundwalk of SPEC §3.7 (receiver interpolation while walking) is M10 material.
- The anechoic clip is a synthetic placeholder (tone bursts, clicks, a noise burst, a sweep; owner replaces it). The clip is re-triggered by the component with a 0.5 s gap (`LoopGapSeconds`) instead of asset looping, so a replacement clip needs no looping flag.
- Assets were created by a python commandlet instead of the editor's MCP server: the toolsets of 5.8.3 have no asset-import / create-asset tool and the `ProgrammaticToolset` sandbox has no `unreal` module. `unreal.AssetTools.create_asset(name, folder, class, None)` uses the null-factory path (`AssetTools.cpp`, `NewObject<UObject>(Pkg, ClassToUse, ...)`).
- The commandlet exits with code 1 because of a pre-existing `LogGameFeatures` error ("Asset manager settings do not include a rule for assets of type GameFeatureData"); the script itself logs "M4 content done" and "Python script executed successfully".
- `EndPlay` resets the shared preset (IR = null) and the submix levels (wet 1, dry 0): in PIE the assets are shared with the editor.

## Listening instructions (for the owner)

Headphones recommended; the clip is mono and not spatialized.

1. Open `Level_Shoebox`, Play (PIE), click **Run** in the panel (top right). The clip starts by itself in the **Dry** variant: the test signal as recorded, no room.
2. Press **2** (Ray). The same signal through the ray-traced room of receiver **R1** (6.7 m from the source, Concrete walls, T30 about 4.7 s): every burst and click is followed by a long, dense, slowly decaying tail; the clicks smear into a "hall"; the gaps between events are filled with reverberation. Press **1** to compare with the dry clip at any moment; the switch is instantaneous.
3. With variant 2 active, press **>** in the receiver selector twice to reach **R3** (1 m in front of the source). Expected difference R1 vs R3: at R3 the direct sound is about 45 times stronger relative to the tail (direct energy 1/4 d^2: 0.0625 vs 0.0014), so the onset of every burst is louder and sharper, the signal sounds closer and clearer, the reverberation is the same length but sits further behind the direct sound (C50 higher by several dB). At R1 (and even more at **R4**, 8.5 m in the far corner) the direct onset is weak, the sound is distant and diffuse, the tail dominates. The absolute loudness of the Ray variant is normalized per receiver (unit-energy convolution), so listen for clarity / distance, not for level.
4. For a shorter room: type `0.3` into **Wall alpha override**, press Enter, **Run** again: the tail collapses to about 0.5 s, the R1 / R3 difference becomes a difference of "room size" rather than of "distance". `0` restores the Concrete room.
5. **Stop** in the Auralization block stops the clip, **Play** restarts it; Tab toggles the cursor if the panel does not react.

Replace the placeholder clip: import a real anechoic recording (speech, 5-10 s, mono) under `Content/Audio/Anechoic/`, set its **Base Submix** to `SM_Auralization`, and point `SimulationManager > Auralization > Anechoic Clip` at it (or re-import over `A_Anechoic_TestSignal`).
