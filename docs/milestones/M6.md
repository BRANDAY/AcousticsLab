# M6 — beam tracing with refraction (SPEC §2.6, §3.4, §3.6)

## Plan

1. **Core, media and interfaces.** `FMedium` gets the presets of the 2012 lens scene (`Glycerol` c 1920 m/s, ρ 1260 kg/m³; `Rubber` c 900 m/s, ρ 930 kg/m³) next to `Air` / `Water`, and `FindMedium(name)` on `FAcousticScene`. Interface triangles already carry `MediumFront` / `MediumBack` / `bInterface` (M1); the extractor fills them (step 6).
2. **Core, refraction math** (`FBeamTracer` statics): `RefractDirection(D, N, c1, c2, Out)` — Snell `sin θ2 / sin θ1 = c2 / c1` with the normal oriented against the incoming direction, returns false for total internal reflection (`sin θ2 > 1`); `CriticalAngle(c1, c2)`; `LeastSquaresApex(origins, directions)` — the point minimizing the sum of squared distances to the three refracted guide lines (`Σ (I − d dᵀ) X = Σ (I − d dᵀ) o`, 3×3 solve; the fallback for parallel lines puts the apex far behind the start triangle and is counted). `FMedium::ReflectionFactor` (M1) gives `R = ((Z2 − Z1) / (Z2 + Z1))²`, `T = 1 − R` (normal incidence, SPEC §2.6).
3. **Core, the refracted beam** (`FBeamTracerSettings::bRefraction`, `SourceMedium`, directional emission `bDirectionalSource` / `SourceDirection` / `SourceConeHalfAngleDeg` for the paper's unidirectional lens source). `FBeamNode` gains `Medium`, `NumRefractions`, `bRefracted`, `bFocusAhead`, `SpreadFactor`. A node hitting an interface triangle spawns **two** children: the reflected one as in M5 (mirrored apex, factor × R, same medium) and, unless one of the three guide rays is totally reflected, the refracted one: start triangle = the parent's cross-section on the interface, guide rays refracted by Snell, apex = least-squares intersection, medium = the other side of the triangle, factor × T. Intensity continuity across the interface (Sikora 2012 Eq. 6): the node factor also takes `(r1 / r_in)²` with `r_in` = parent apex → barycentre of the start triangle and `r1` = new apex → the same barycentre, so the energy density at the start triangle is the incoming one times T whatever the new apex is (for reflections the ratio is 1). The refracted beam may **converge** (apex ahead of the start triangle: the lens): the node is then the nappe between the start triangle and the focus (`bFocusAhead`, containment test on the antipodal cone, candidates sorted far-to-near from the apex, occlusion rays cast along the propagation direction), and a beam that reaches its focus without hitting anything continues beyond it as a diverging node of the same order (`bContinuation`). Interface triangles ignore the material absorption (energy split by impedances only, SPEC §2.6). Delays: `FBeamTree::TracePath` also reports the medium of every path segment and `QueryReceivers` sums `|segment| / c(medium)`; the spreading distance stays the apex distance (the paper's r2). Order counts reflections and refractions alike (paper: "reflections/refractions up to 4th order").
4. **Core tests** (`Private/Tests/RefractionTests.cpp`): `AcousticsLab.Core.Geometry.Snell` (air↔water angles, normal incidence unchanged, plane of incidence, critical angle 13.40°, TIR just above it), `AcousticsLab.Core.Geometry.LeastSquaresApex` (concurrent lines exact, skew lines: residual smaller than at perturbed points, parallel fallback), `AcousticsLab.Core.Solvers.RefractionEnergy` (R + T = 1 for every medium pair; on an air/water interface every reflected child has factor R, every refracted child T·(r1/r_in)², beams beyond the critical angle have no refracted child, a receiver straight below the source gets the transmitted arrival at `h_air / c_air + h_water / c_water` ± 0.1 ms), `AcousticsLab.Core.Solvers.RefractedBeamTreeDeterminism` (two builds bit-identical, brute force == BVH, single-receiver runs == combined run, with refraction on), `AcousticsLab.Core.Solvers.LensFocus` (sphere lens of the paper, scaled ×10: the receiver on the axis inside the back of the sphere gets more energy than one 0.3 m off the axis). All M0–M5 tests stay green.
5. **Game, settings / manager / HUD.** `FSolverSettings::bRefraction` is wired into `ToBeamTracerSettings()`; `AAcousticSource` gets `MediumName` (medium the source sits in), `bDirectional`, `ConeHalfAngleDeg` (forward vector = the actor's X); the manager resolves the source medium by name and passes the directional cone; the HUD gets a **Source** selector (SourceId cycling, the level has two), a **Refraction** checkbox, the stats "refracted / TIR / continued" and the fixed note *"Refracted beam apex = least-squares intersection of the 3 refracted guide rays (approximation, Sikora 2012 §3.1.2 / Sikora 2010)"* while refraction is on. `SubdivisionLevel` is allowed up to 7 (the lens needs dense beams; with the cone only the beams inside it are traced).
6. **Game, extraction of media.** Decision (noted here, SPEC §3.4 only says "mesh with the tag Interface"): a component (or actor) tagged `Interface` yields interface triangles; the media are named by the tags `AcMediumFront:<name>` (the side the UE face normal points to) and `AcMediumBack:<name>`; names resolve through `AcousticMediumPresets` (Air, Water, Glycerol, Rubber) or the extractor's `MediaLibrary` (`FAcousticMediumSpec { Name, SpeedOfSound, Density }`), medium 0 is always Air. A material asset with `bInterface` (WaterSurface) also marks an interface; without medium tags the defaults are front Air / back Water with a warning. Non-interface triangles get `MediumFront = MediumBack = 0`.
7. **Game, Level_Refraction** (`ARefractionDemoActor`, placed through the editor's MCP server by `Tools/Editor/create_m6_content.py`): (a) **pool** 20 × 10 × 3 m — floor and four Concrete walls (single-sided quads like the shoebox), water surface at 2 m as an interface quad (front Air, back Water, material WaterSurface, no collision), source `Pool` at 1.5 m above the water, receiver grid `P` 18 × 8 × 1 m at 1 m under the surface, point receivers `R1` (under the source) / `R2` (5 m aside under water); (b) **lens** 60 m away on +Y: the 2012 scene scaled ×10 — a rubber sphere of diameter 1 m (`FSphereGenerator`, GeometryCore, as a `UDynamicMeshComponent`; front Glycerol, back Rubber) centred 7.5 m from a directional source `Lens` (cone 4.5°) in glycerol, a vertical receiver grid `L` in the axial plane through the sphere (0.1 m tiles, receivers at the tile centres) and point receivers `R3` (paraxial focus inside the back of the sphere) / `R4` (behind the sphere). ## Deviations

From SPEC §2.6 / the plan:
- The apex of a refracted beam is the **least-squares point** of the three refracted guide lines (they are not concurrent after refraction); the reflected beam keeps the exact mirrored apex. Shown in the HUD note and in README.
- Energy continuity across the interface uses the ratio `(r1 / r_in)²` of the apex distances to the entry triangle (Sikora 2012 Eq. 6), so the transmitted energy density at the interface equals `T` times the incoming one whatever the fitted apex is. `R`, `T` are the normal-incidence impedance factors for every angle (SPEC §2.6); the material absorption of an interface is ignored.
- Total internal reflection is decided per beam by its **axis** (Sikora 2012 §3.3.4: only a reflected beam when the incoming angle of the divided beam is greater than the critical angle); the guide rays of a refracting beam that lie beyond the critical angle are clamped to a refraction angle of asin(0.999) = 87.4° so the beam stays usable. A refracted beam whose pieces are all slivers leaves no node (the counter `NumRefracted` may exceed the node count by one or two).
- The directional source is an emission mask (root beams whose axis lies inside the cone), not a directivity pattern; the direct sound outside the cone is marked occluded.
- The ray tracer and the image-source solver treat interface triangles as ordinary walls (no media, no refraction); air absorption is applied in every medium.
- Pool and lens share one level 60 m apart (menu item 1.2 opens both); the lens scene is the paper's one scaled ×10 (sphere radius 0.5 m, source 7.5 m away, cone 4.5°) because beams of a few centimetres would need levels beyond 7. The paper's refocusing by intersecting edge-ray pairs is replaced by the least-squares apex; reflections / refractions are limited by `MaxBeamOrder` (3 in the level; the paper uses 4).
- With a slower medium inside the sphere (rubber 900 m/s in glycerol 1920 m/s) the ball-lens focus lies inside the back of the sphere (`n R / (2 (n − 1))` = 0.47 m from the centre for n = 2.13), so the receiver grid `L` is the vertical axial plane through the sphere and `R3` sits 0.35 m behind the centre; `R4` behind the sphere sees the diverging beams after the second refraction.

## How to verify in the editor

1. Open `Level_Refraction`, select **SimulationManager**: `Solver = BeamTracer`, `Settings.bRefraction = true`, `BeamSubdivisionLevel = 4`, `MaxBeamOrder = 3`, `SourceId = Pool`; PIE, press **Run**. The HUD shows "Refraction: N refracted beams, N total internal reflections …"; beams above the water keep the order palette, beams under water are cyan / blue / violet; beams arriving flatter than 13.4° only reflect (no cyan child). The heatmap `P` under water shows the total level, brightest under the source.
2. Switch the **Source** selector to `Lens` (or `SourceId = Lens`), `BeamSubdivisionLevel = 6`, press **Run**: the cone of 110 root beams from the glycerol source refracts into the rubber sphere, the beams converge inside its back half, the vertical heatmap `L` shows the focal spot on the axis and `R3` (0.05 m) reports refracted arrivals only. Set the Source back to `Pool` afterwards.
3. Toggle the **Refraction** checkbox off and run again: the surface and the sphere behave as ordinary walls (no cyan beams, no counters).
4. Scripted: `python Tools/Editor/create_m6_content.py` (idempotent) and `python Tools/Editor/verify_m6_pie.py --shots docs/milestones/img` with the editor started with `-ModelContextProtocolStartServer -ModelContextProtocolPort=8000`.
